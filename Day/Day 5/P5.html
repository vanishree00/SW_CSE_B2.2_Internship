The Indian Air Force (IAF) is the air arm of the Indian Armed Forces, globally recognized as the third-most powerful air force according to the WDMMA 2026 Rankings. Commanded by the Chief of the Air Staff, Air Chief Marshal AP Singh, its primary mission is to secure Indian airspace and conduct aerial warfare.Key Statistics & OverviewMotto: "Nabhah Sprsham Diptam" (Touch the Sky with Glory), derived from the Bhagavad Gita.Established: 8 October 1932.Personnel Strength: Approximately 170,000 active personnel.Fleet Size: Over 1,700 aircraft operating across more than 60 airbases.Global Standing: Ranks 3rd globally, ahead of China's PLAAF.Active Fighter Fleet & ModernisationThe IAF operates a highly diverse fleet structured into approximately 29 fighter squadrons.Sukhoi Su-30MKI: The twin-engine, multirole air superiority mainstay of the fleet.Dassault Rafale: Advanced 4.5-generation fighter jet handling deep-strike missions.HAL Tejas: India's indigenous delta-wing Light Combat Aircraft (LCA).MiG-29 & Mirage 2000: Proven multirole combat aircraft optimized for interception and electronic warfare.To maintain its competitive edge, India is aggressively pursuing a multi-billion dollar mega-deal to acquire 114 Multi-Role Fighter Aircraft (MRFA), with France recently submitting a comprehensive proposal for large-scale local manufacturing of Rafale jets under the "Make in India" initiative.Emerging Tactical Focus: Anti-Drone WarfareResponding to changing modern warfare dynamics, the IAF has initiated a structural shift to convert its existing helicopter fleet into specialized counter-unmanned aerial systems (C-UAS) units. During the recent Exercise Rotor Clap, platforms like the Apache AH-64E, Mi-35, and the indigenous Prachand attack helicopter successfully integrated tactical anti-drone drills. The IAF is actively procuring micro-missiles, proximity-detonating steel pellet warheads, forward-mounted guns, and AI-enhanced target identification systems to intercept hostile rogue drones.Women in Combat LeadershipThe IAF continues to pioneer gender integration within the combat streams of the Indian military. Notably, Squadron Leader Bhawana Kanth—one of India's first female fighter pilots—made history by becoming the first woman to graduate from the IAF's elite "Top Gun" school, securing her qualification as a Fighter Combat Leader.
